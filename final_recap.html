Two Stars, Two Records
KYLIAN MBAPPE
Centre Forward :: #10 :: Real Madrid
He arrived at his first World Cup as a teenager with pace to burn. He leaves this one as the greatest goalscorer the tournament has ever seen - 22 goals across three tournaments, and the first ever footballer to win back-to-back Golden Boots. Every team that has approached him has tried to stop him, and none of them fully have. This time: 10 goals from 41 shots, a number that barely slowed down all summer. He closes out the 25/26 season as the top scorer across three separate competitions at once - 25 goals in La Liga, 15 in the Champions League, and now 10 at the World Cup.

MICHAEL OLISE
Attacking Midfielder :: #11 :: Bayern Munich
This was Olise's first World Cup, and he used it to break a record that had stood since Pele in 1970 - 7 assists, the most anyone has ever managed at a single tournament (5 for Mbappe alone, plus one each for Dembele and Barcola). He took 20 shots himself and let every one of them pass, always choosing the teammate in space over the glory. Defenders brought him down 16 times trying to stop the service; he barely fouled anyone back. He ends the 25/26 season as the top assist provider across three separate competitions - 19 in the Bundesliga, 6 in the Champions League, and now 7 at the World Cup.

France finish the tournament in 4th place - six wins from six, then two losses in a row: the Semi Final to Spain (0-2), and the third-place match to England (4-6). Spain went on to beat Argentina 1-0 in extra time and lift the trophy.

Two numbers matter here, and they answer different questions. 51.1% was France's chance of winning that one Semi Final match - basically a coin flip. 28.1% was their chance of winning the whole tournament, which meant winning two matches in a row, not one. It was the best number of any team left in the competition - but still under 50%, because winning twice back to back is always harder than winning once.

France had the best underlying numbers of the four semifinalists - the sharpest attack, the tightest defense, the most complete squad on paper. Some teams get remembered for what they won. This one deserves to be remembered for how well it played.
